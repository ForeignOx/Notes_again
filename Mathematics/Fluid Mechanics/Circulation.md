We know that [[vorticity|vorticity]] is local, if we want to quantify how much rotation there is in a region, vorticity alone is not good enough, we need circulation
Let $C$ be a simple, smooth, closed curve in the fluid, then the circulation around $C$ is
$$
\Gamma=\oint_{C}\underline{u}\cdot d\underline{x}
$$
It counts how much $\underline{u}$ is in the direction of the curve, as we go around the curve
$S$ denotes a surface spanning $C$, then Stokes' theorem gives
$$
\gamma=\oint_{C}\underline{u}\cdot d\underline{x}=\int _{S}\underline{\nabla} \times \underline{u}  \cdot d\underline{S} =\int _{S}\underline{\omega} \cdot d\underline{S} 
$$
So the circulation is a measure of the overall rotation of the fluid in a certain region
