A spacecurve $\underline{x}(t)$ represents a 3d [[Vectors|vector]] $(\underline{x}_{x},\underline{x}_{y},\underline{x}_{z})$ depending [[Continuity|continuously]] on $t\in[a,b]$
We consider such curves as ssmooth if $\underline{x}$ is at least $\mathcal{C}^{3}$ [[Differentiation|differentiable]] for all $t$
A common requirement for spacecurves parametrised by their [[Arclength|arclengths]] is that $\underline{x}(s)$ is smooth over the period $[0,L]$, where $L$ is the total arclength of $\underline{x}$, and $\underline{x}(t=a)=\underline{x}(s=0)$, and similarly $\underline{x}(t=b)=\underline{x}(s=L)$
## Open vs Closed
Closed spacecurves represent the set of curves whose endpoints are equal, $\underline{x}(a)=\underline{x}(b)$, and for which all derivatives agree at $a$ and $b$
Open curves will be those for which $\underline{x}(a)\neq \underline{x}(b)$
A commonly used subset of open curves are those for which $\underline{x}'(a)=\underline{x}'(b)$
## Concatenation
Consider two section of spacecurve $\underline{x}_{1}$ and $\underline{x}_{2}$, the smooth attachment of $\underline{x}_{1}$ and $\underline{x}_{2}$ is denoted as $\underline{x}_{1}+\underline{x}_{2}$. Thiss indicates there is at least one point $p$ at which the curves mare joined, such that $\underline{x}'_{1}(t)=\underline{x}_{2}'(t)$ at $p$
